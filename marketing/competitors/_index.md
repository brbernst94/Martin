# Competitive landscape

*Owner: Marty. Last full sweep: 2026-09-16. Marty refreshes one competitor file
per week and re-sweeps the landscape monthly.*

## The shape of the market

The monthly-art-print category is real, established, and **crowded at $10–15**.
Dozens of individual artists run print clubs off Shopify with near-identical
mechanics: one small print, a letter from the artist, nice envelope, cancel
anytime, ~$10–12/month including shipping.

That price band is a trap for us. At $12 with worldwide shipping included, the
economics only work if the artist is printing digitally at scale and treating it
as audience-building rather than a business. Patrick makes physical prints by
hand plus a sticker plus a third piece. We cannot be the cheap option and
shouldn't try.

## Where the actual gap is

Three gaps, in order of how much they're worth to us:

1. **Nobody owns food and drink.** The clubs are general-illustration or
   nature/figurative. Cocktail, coffee, and restaurant-industry buyers are
   served by print-on-demand marketplaces, not by anyone with a point of view.
   This is our wedge.
2. **Nobody is selling the process.** Almost every competitor sells a finished
   image. A working press, plates, separations, and misregistration is a story
   none of them can tell. It is also, conveniently, the best short-form video
   content in the category.
3. **Everybody ships one thing.** One print, one envelope, predictable.
   Three-piece bundles with a variable third element are rare at this price, and
   the variability is exactly what defends against the "I have enough prints"
   cancel.

## Where we're exposed

- **Price.** We will be 2–4x the category norm. Every piece of positioning has
  to earn that gap explicitly.
- **Free worldwide shipping is the category default.** Competitors at $10–12
  include it. We have three physical objects and real postage, so we either
  build it into price or look worse on the comparison.
- **They all have audiences already.** These clubs are monetizing followings
  built over years. We are starting at zero. That's the real gap, not the
  product.

## Adjacent, and worth watching

Not print clubs, but competing for the same dollar and the same wall:
- **SnackCrate** — food subscription box. Notable because Patrick has designed
  for them; useful reference for the unboxing standard in this category.
- **Beer/coffee subscription clubs** — same buyer, same "monthly arrives in the
  mail" habit, different object. A potential partnership channel, not a threat.
- **Bar-cart and barware DTC brands** — competing directly for the wall space
  and the discretionary spend.

## Marty's read

We are not competing with the $10 print clubs, and we should stop thinking of
them as competitors. They're a different product for a different buyer. Our real
competition for the $35-ish monthly discretionary dollar is a bottle of decent
amaro, a couple of good cocktails out, or a coffee subscription. Positioning
should be built against *that* comparison, not against a cheaper print.
