# Print-on-demand marketplaces

*Fine Art America, iCanvas, Society6, Great Big Canvas, Printler, Fy!, Etsy.
Checked 2026-09-16.*

## Why they're in this file

They aren't subscription businesses, but they are where our customer goes today
when they think "I want a cocktail print for above the bar cart." Fine Art
America alone lists cocktail prints from thousands of artists. If our positioning
is "affordable art for your walls," this is who we lose to, instantly and
permanently.

## What they beat us on

- **Selection.** Infinite. Any subject, any size, any format, today.
- **Speed.** Buy it now, on the wall this week. No monthly wait.
- **Price at the bottom.** Unbeatable for a poster.
- **SEO.** They own every commercial query in the category. This is why
  `channels.md` keeps Search off until we have a shop and a brand.

## What they lose on — comprehensively

- **No maker.** Nobody knows who made it and nobody cares. There's no
  relationship, no signature, no story.
- **No object quality.** Giclée on demand versus a hand-pulled print on real
  paper is not a comparison once you hold both.
- **No curation, so no discovery.** Infinite selection means the buyer does all
  the work. Ours arrives with an idea already attached.
- **No recurrence.** They have transactions; we have a habit.

## The strategic consequence

We must never compete on "buy a print." The moment our marketing sounds like a
poster shop we're in a search-and-price fight against companies with infinite
inventory and real SEO budgets.

Every piece of copy should be about **the maker and the month**, never about the
image as a purchasable product. The customer isn't buying a print — they're
subscribing to a person.
