# Morning brief — 2026-09-16

**The one thing:** The $10–15 monthly print club category is saturated and we
should stop treating it as our competition — our real competitor for a $35
discretionary monthly spend is a bottle of amaro, and that's a much easier fight.

## What I found

- **The category's price floor is real and low.** Ravi Zupa's Friendly Monthly
  Print Club is $10/mo for a signed 5.5×8" print with free *worldwide* shipping;
  Andrea Jacobsen's is $12/mo for a 5×7 postcard print with an original poem on
  the back. Both include a personal letter. At those prices with that postage,
  they're printing digitally at volume and running the club as audience-building.
  We can't and shouldn't play there.
  (ravizupa.com, andreajacobsen.com — checked today)

- **Two mechanics worth stealing outright.** Zupa runs a lottery — any month's
  envelope might contain an original. Jacobsen lets subscribers **skip** a month,
  not just cancel. The lottery makes people open the envelope immediately; the
  skip converts a cancel into a pause. Both cost close to nothing and I've
  written them into `retention.md`.

- **Nobody in this category owns food and drink.** The clubs are general
  illustration or nature. Food-and-drink buyers currently get served by
  print-on-demand marketplaces with infinite selection and zero point of view.
  That's our wedge, and it's wider than I expected.

- **The discount finding, which changes our launch plan.** Subscribers acquired
  through heavy discounting — free first box, 50% off — churn at 2–3x the rate of
  full-price buyers. At our margins that's negative-value acquisition. If we need
  a launch incentive it should be an extra *piece*, never a lower price.
  (eightx.co — checked today)

- **Pinterest is the most underrated channel available to us and I'd bet on it
  harder than Instagram.** It's search-driven, so a pin still pulls traffic in
  two years, and "bar cart art" is a large evergreen query. Instagram posts are
  dead in 48 hours. Same creative, radically different half-life.
