# Guerrilla playbook

*Owner: Marty. 2026-09-16.*

Most DTC brands have to manufacture marketing collateral separately from their
product. We don't. **We make small, desirable, food-and-drink-themed printed
objects.** That is the same sentence as "we make the best guerrilla marketing
assets in our category." Every tactic below exploits that.

Ranked by leverage per dollar. Nothing here executes without Brian's go-ahead;
several of these need a city (see `company/open-questions.md` #4).

---

## 1. Coasters in bars — the flagship tactic

**The move:** Print a run of beautiful two-color coasters. Not branded flyers
with a logo — actual good-looking coasters a bar would be happy to use. Small
mark and a short URL on the back. Give them to bars for free, by hand.

**Why it's unfair:** A cocktail bar's clientele *is* our primary ICP, sitting
still, holding a drink, with nothing to look at. The coaster lives under their
glass for 40 minutes. And because it's genuinely good, the bar wants it — this
is a gift, not a flyer, so it doesn't get thrown away.

- **Bet:** 2,000 coasters into 15 bars produces 200+ site visits and 50+ waitlist
  signups at a cost under $400. Sub-$8 acquisition cost on our best segment.
- **Cost:** ~$300–400 printing, ~6 Brian-hours of walking into bars, ~4
  Patrick-hours of design.
- **Kill date:** 60 days after the first drop. Track with a unique short link.

## 2. Seed the industry — 30 free prints, no ask

**The move:** Identify 30 named bartenders, cooks, baristas, and sommeliers.
Mail each one a print, hand-addressed, with a note from Patrick. No discount
code, no "would you post this," no ask of any kind.

**Why it works:** These people work in rooms full of our other customers, and
they are congenitally suspicious of being marketed to. Removing the ask is the
entire tactic. A meaningful fraction post it unprompted, and the ones who hang it
at work are a permanent billboard.

- **Bet:** 30 prints → 8+ organic posts → 2,000+ impressions on exactly the right
  audience, plus 3–5 ongoing relationships.
- **Cost:** ~$300 all-in. The highest-leverage money in the entire plan.
- **Kill date:** None. If it works, do it every quarter.

## 3. Stickers as distribution, not swag

**The move:** Every bundle ships with two of that month's sticker. One for the
subscriber, one to give away. Say so on the card.

**Why it works:** Stickers are the only marketing asset that a customer will
place in public for us, for free, permanently. We already make them. Doubling the
count costs almost nothing.

- **Bet:** 15% of subscribers place the second sticker somewhere public.
- **Cost:** Marginal print cost only.
- **Kill date:** None — measure via sticker-specific short URL or QR.

## 4. The mailer is the ad

**The move:** Design the outer mailer so that the *unopened package* is worth
photographing. Printed exterior, visible from the mail pile, obviously not from
Amazon.

**Why it matters:** Unboxing is the only moment in a subscription where the
customer is guaranteed to be paying full attention and holding a phone. If the
package is boring, we lose the single best organic content opportunity we get,
twelve times a year, per customer.

- **Bet:** A designed mailer takes organic unboxing posts from ~2% of subscribers
  to ~10%.
- **Cost:** Patrick design time + print. Must be decided before launch — it's a
  packaging decision, not a marketing one.
- **Kill date:** N/A, this is a product decision.

## 5. Bathroom-wall placements

**The move:** The framed print in the bathroom of a good bar or coffee shop, with
a small credit card next to it. Trade: they get free art, we get the wall.

**Why it works:** Hyper-local, context-specific, and it's a captive audience with
a phone already out. Costs one print per venue.

- **Bet:** 10 venues → 30+ signups/quarter.
- **Cost:** ~$100 in prints and frames, Brian's legwork.
- **Kill date:** 90 days, measured by QR scans.

## 6. Menu and recipe-card collabs

**The move:** Offer a local bar or restaurant a free printed menu insert or
recipe card, designed by Patrick, carrying a small credit. They get design work
they can't afford; we get our object in the hands of every customer that night.

**Why it works:** It's the third-piece format anyway, so it doubles as R&D for
the product. And it makes Patrick's actual professional skill the marketing.

- **Bet:** 3 venues in 90 days, 500+ impressions each per week.
- **Cost:** Real Patrick-hours — 4–6 per collab. Only do this if the venue is
  well-matched and will actually use it.
- **Kill date:** After 3 attempts, if none converts, stop.

## 7. Print-the-city series

**The move:** A print of a specific, beloved, hyper-local thing — the one dish at
the one restaurant everyone in town argues about. Post it tagging the venue.

**Why it works:** Local pride is the most reliable organic engagement driver
there is, and the venue almost always reshares to an audience that overlaps ours
perfectly. Free distribution from a real institution.

- **Bet:** One per month; at least one gets reshared by an account with 10k+
  local followers.
- **Cost:** It's a print we were making anyway — just choose the subject with
  distribution in mind.
- **Kill date:** None. Bake it into the editorial calendar.

## 8. The misprint sale

**The move:** Printmaking produces rejects. Sell them cheap, occasionally,
unannounced, to the email list only.

**Why it works:** Converts the waitlist into buyers at low risk, creates urgency
honestly, produces revenue from waste, and reinforces the "these are physically
made" message better than any copy could. Also an excellent retention offer for
existing subscribers.

- **Bet:** Each drop converts 5% of the list.
- **Cost:** Zero — this is scrap.
- **Kill date:** None.

## Explicitly rejected

- **Wheatpasting / unsanctioned stickering.** Illegal in most places, and the
  brand is "a craftsman you'd invite into your home." Vandalism is off-brand and
  a legal liability for a business with a real address.
- **Fake accounts, astroturfing, seeded reviews.** Our audience includes Reddit
  communities that detect this instantly and punish it permanently.
- **Buying an existing food/drink meme account.** Audience is real, but the
  followers aren't buyers and the association cheapens the work.
