# Business brief

*Last updated: 2026-09-16 — from Brian's founding description, updated with
Patrick's intake answers the same day. Anything marked `OPEN` is not yet
decided; see `company/open-questions.md`.*

## The product

A monthly mail bundle of original artwork, themed around **food and drink**.
Every month has one theme and all four pieces sit inside it — Patrick's own
example is a Christmas cocktail on the card and a pie on the print.

Each month a subscriber receives:
1. **A hand-made relief print** — the anchor of the bundle. Open edition, signed
   and numbered. Same paper every month. Subject is food: a vegetable, a fruit,
   a dish.
2. **A cocktail card** — the month's featured drink, its recipe, and a graphic.
   Designed to be collected into a cocktail book over time. Patrick's idea. This
   is his graphic design work rather than his printmaking.
3. **A letter** — why he chose this month's subject, what's new in the studio,
   and a request that subscribers send photos of where their pieces ended up.
4. **A sticker** — using one element of that month's cocktail or food graphic,
   not a copy of the card.

All artwork is made by **Patrick Selner**, printmaker and graphic designer.
Nothing in the bundle is licensed, drop-shipped, or AI-generated.

**Everything ships flat, in an envelope.** Patrick ruled out coasters,
matchbooks, tea towels and similar objects on 2026-09-16 — they don't fit the
mailer, they push shipping cost up on weight, and they cost too much per unit.
The founding brief's "surprise piece that changes every month" is retired; see
`company/decisions.md`.

## Why the card and the letter matter more than they look

The cocktail card is the **retention product**: an unfinished collection is a
reason not to cancel, which is why it ships every month without gaps.

The letter is the **community product**, and it does the acquisition work the
retired surprise piece used to do. Patrick is the one person in the company
genuinely excited to talk to subscribers, and asking every month for photos of
where the pieces ended up gives us a stream of real customers' walls and bar
carts — better content than anything we'd make, at zero cost.

What we lost with the surprise piece is unpredictability. What's unpredictable
now is the *theme* — which drink, which food — not the object. That's a thinner
hook and marketing has to work harder for it. See `marketing/retention.md`.

## The model

Physical subscription box, monthly, direct to consumer. Recurring revenue,
physical COGS, real shipping costs, real churn.

**Launch: 11/1/26.** First bundle delivered **12/1/26**.
**December's print and cocktail card 01 must exist and be photographed by 10/25**
or there's nothing to launch with. Patrick says he can work about a month ahead
if the planning is done in time.
**Based in Denver, Colorado.**
**Price: $32/month proposed** ($320/year), pending Brian's sign-off — see
`marketing/pricing.md`.

`OPEN` — brand name, storefront platform, shipping regions, paper stock by name.

### Unit economics (from Brian's breakeven analysis, 2026-09-16)

Variable cost per bundle is **$4.65** before card processing — paper $0.80, ink
$0.65, sticker $0.75, envelope $0.45, shipping $2.00. Fixed costs are **$43/month**
(website $23, relief blocks $10, parchment $10).

Three caveats that matter more than the numbers: **shipping at $2.00 is still
unverified against a real bundle at a counter** (`#14`), **neither the cocktail
card nor the letter is costed** — two printed items missing from the sheet, both
adding material and weight — and **Patrick's labour is not in the model**. He
can't give a per-bundle time because batches are far faster per unit, so labour
goes in per batch once he clocks a run (`#3`).

## What we have going for us

- **A real artist with real range.** Patrick's portfolio is built around food
  and drink brand work: Snackcrate, Counter Culture, Olio, Seed And Stone,
  Sidekicks, The Great Globetrotter, Pollinator Corridor.
  (https://selnerpatrick.myportfolio.com/, checked 2026-09-16.)
- **He has shipped in this exact category before.** Snackcrate is a snack
  subscription box. Patrick has already designed for the unboxing moment.
- **Both his trades are in the bundle.** The print is the printmaker, the card
  is the graphic designer. That's more product for the same person.
- **Capacity is not the ceiling.** He says a few thousand bundles a month before
  it stops being fun, starting at 50–100 and doubling. Demand is the constraint,
  not the studio.
- **A narrow, legible theme.** "Food and drink" is a category with existing
  obsessive communities — cocktail people, coffee people, natural wine people,
  hot sauce people, restaurant-industry people — who already buy prints, already
  decorate around the theme, and are already gathered in findable places.
- **The product photographs itself.** Printmaking on camera is inherently good
  content, and filming happens inside work already underway, so it costs
  nothing extra.

## What is working against us

- **Discretionary spend, monthly commitment.** Every cancel is one click, and
  the bundle is not a necessity.
- **The category is crowded at the bottom.** A large number of $10–20/month
  print clubs already exist. Differentiation has to be real, not positioning
  copy. See `marketing/competitors/`.
- **Four hand-made items every month, forever.** The card and the letter can't
  skip — one breaks a collection, the other breaks the relationship. Whether
  that's sustainable is open (`#18`), and it's the reason Marty did not fight
  for the surprise piece.
- **The bundle is all flat paper now.** Every piece is something to look at.
  Nothing in it gets *used*, which is the thing that would have kept a
  wall-space-limited subscriber from cancelling. Live risk, handled in
  `marketing/retention.md`.
- **Six weeks to launch with no name and almost no audience.** Patrick has 250
  followers on his art Instagram, 1,200 on a personal account that isn't about
  art, and a handful of Etsy sales to friends and family. No list. The first 100
  subscribers will not come from advertising — they'll come from Denver, by hand.
- **Patrick doesn't want the marketing and finance side**, and said so. Anything
  that depends on him selling will not happen. Anything that depends on him
  talking to people about the work will.

## The honest strategic read

The differentiator is not "monthly art print." It is **a working printmaker's
food-and-drink studio practice, delivered**. The competition is mostly
illustrators mailing digital prints of digital work. Patrick makes physical
objects with physical process. That process — the presses, the prints that come
out wrong, the test prints — is the marketing asset and the moat.

Sell the studio, not the subscription.
