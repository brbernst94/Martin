# Glossary

*For Brian. The strategy docs use these terms; Marty shouldn't use them in Slack
without translating. If he does, tell him.*

## Money and customers

**CAC — customer acquisition cost.** What it costs to get one paying subscriber.
Total marketing spend ÷ new subscribers. Category average is about $72.

**Churn.** The share of subscribers who cancel in a month. At 8% monthly churn
the average person stays 12.5 months; at 4%, 25 months. Halving churn is worth
more than any channel decision.

**Voluntary vs. involuntary churn.** Voluntary is someone choosing to cancel.
Involuntary is their card failing. Involuntary is a quarter to a third of all
cancellations and it's fixable with software.

**Dunning.** The emails and retries that recover a failed payment. Card expiry
warnings, retry schedule, one-click update link.

**LTV — lifetime value.** Total profit from one subscriber before they leave.
Roughly: monthly price × margin ÷ churn rate.

**LTV:CAC.** What you get back for each dollar spent acquiring someone. Below
3:1 the model doesn't work. 5:1 is excellent.

**CAC payback.** How many months of subscription it takes to earn back what you
spent acquiring that person. At $32 it's about 2.8 months; at $15 it's 7.5.

**MRR — monthly recurring revenue.** Active subscribers × average price.

**ARPU — average revenue per user.** Average monthly revenue per subscriber,
after any discounts.

**Contribution margin.** What's left from one subscription after the costs that
scale with it — paper, ink, postage, card fees. Not counting fixed costs.

**Cohort.** Everyone who subscribed in the same month, tracked as a group.
Matters because average churn hides the fact that one month's subscribers might
be leaving much faster than another's.

## Getting found

**Impressions.** How many times something was shown to someone. Not how many
people saw it, and not how many cared.

**Reach.** How many distinct people saw it.

**Engagement rate.** The share of people who saw something and did something —
liked, commented, saved, clicked.

**Organic vs. paid.** Organic is free — posts, search results, word of mouth.
Paid is advertising.

**Top of funnel / bottom of funnel.** Top is people who've never heard of us.
Bottom is people about to buy. Different messages work at each end.

**Conversion rate.** The share of people who take the next step. Site visitors
who join the list; list members who subscribe.

**Attribution.** Knowing which thing actually made someone buy. Genuinely hard at
our size, which is why one "how did you hear about us?" question at checkout
beats any analytics dashboard we could install.

**UTM.** Tracking codes appended to a link so you can tell which post or coaster
sent someone.

**Evergreen.** Content that keeps working for months or years. Pinterest is
evergreen; Instagram is not.

## Printmaking

*Patrick's vocabulary. Useful for reading the docs — Marty should never make you
decode these.*

**Pull.** Printing one copy by hand. "A pull" is one print coming off the press.

**Proof.** A test print made while setting up, before the real run.

**Registration.** Whether the colors line up. **Misregistration** is when they
don't — sometimes a mistake, sometimes the point.

**Screenprint.** Ink pushed through a fine mesh screen with a squeegee, one color
at a time.

**Relief print / block print.** Carving away everything that shouldn't print,
inking what's left, pressing it to paper. The "relief blocks" in the cost sheet.

**Reduction print.** One block carved away further between each color, so the
edition can never be reprinted.

**Spot color.** A specific pre-mixed ink, rather than a color built from CMYK
dots. Gives the flat, saturated look.

**Edition.** The set of prints made from one setup. A **limited edition** has a
stated number and each print is numbered (12/50). An **open edition** doesn't.

**Stock / rag.** The paper. "Coventry Rag" is a cotton paper. Paper choice is
most of why a print feels expensive.
