## What this actually tells us

**He is a food-and-drink designer, commercially and repeatedly.** Not a
coincidence of project names — Counter Culture, Olio, SnackCrate, Cadbury,
Nestlé, Poulain, Brets, OLW. National FMCG brands. That credential is real and
we have been under-selling it.

**The Cocktail Series is the product, already.** A numbered series, one drink per
piece, flat and bold, recipe set small at the bottom. That is the monthly print
with the recipe on it. It exists and it works.

**SnackCrate proves the third piece.** Those country booklets are exactly the
format the variable third piece needs — editorial, printed, collectible, and he
has shipped them for an actual subscription box.

**He works flat, saturated and type-forward.** Limited palettes, high contrast,
no texture. He also switches display faces per project rather than carrying one
signature face, which means the brand's type system has to be decided
deliberately rather than inherited from his portfolio.

## What's still missing

None of these ten show printmaking — no press, plate, pulled print or ink on
paper. Brian confirmed on 2026-09-22 that the hand-printed work exists, so the
"working printmaker" positioning holds.

Which makes this a content gap rather than a strategy risk, and a bigger one.
The press, the plates, a pulled print and a botched registration are the process
videos, the Pinterest set, and the only real proof of why this costs $32 when a
print club costs $12. It is the most valuable material the company has and
nobody has photographed it.

Wanted before the first Instagram post, not after. Open question #17.
