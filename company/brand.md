# Brand

## Positioning statement

> For people who take what they eat and drink seriously enough to hang it on the
> wall, we mail a working printmaker's studio practice — a print, a cocktail
> card, a letter, and a sticker, all on one idea — every month.
>
> Not a print club. A standing invitation into Patrick Selner's studio.

## The one-sentence version

**Original food-and-drink art, made by hand, in your mailbox every month.**

## What we are not

- Not a curated box of other people's art.
- Not digital art printed on demand.
- Not "affordable art for your walls" — that's the whole bottom of the market
  and it's a price fight we lose.
- Not a surprise-and-delight mystery box. The contents are the same four things
  every month. What changes is the idea.

## Brand pillars

1. **The hand.** Everything is made, not generated. Ink, plates, paper, colors
   that either line up or don't. Where possible we show the making, not just the
   made.
2. **The subject.** Food and drink, treated with the seriousness normally
   reserved for landscapes and portraits. A negroni deserves a good print.
3. **The collection.** The cocktail cards accumulate into a book the subscriber
   builds themselves. The bundle isn't twelve unrelated deliveries, it's one
   thing being assembled over a year.
4. **The name on it.** This is one person's practice, not a faceless studio.
   Patrick is the brand, he writes the letter in his own voice, and he answers
   the people who write back. Marketing should put him on camera and in the copy.

*(The old pillar 3, "the surprise," is retired — the variable fourth piece was
cut on 2026-09-16. See `company/decisions.md`.)*

## Voice

Confident, dry, specific. The voice of a good bartender or a good line cook —
knows their craft, doesn't oversell it, funnier than expected. The letter is the
purest expression of it and it's Patrick's own writing, not copy we produce for
him.

**Do:**
- Name things precisely. "Two-color relief print on [stock]" beats "beautiful
  high-quality art print." The stock still needs naming.
- Talk about the process and the failures. A ruined print is content.
- Have opinions about food and drink. The brand should be a *voice in the
  category*, not a vendor to it.

**Don't:**
- "Elevate your space." "Curated." "Artisanal." "Journey." "Delight."
- Exclamation points in body copy.
- Describe the art as "stunning." Let people decide that.

## The sticker rule

The sticker is one element lifted out of the month's cocktail or food graphic —
never a copy of the card, because the same picture twice in one envelope makes
four items feel like three. It has to read at an inch on a laptop lid and work
with no context, because whoever sees it on a stranger's water bottle has never
heard of us. Our mark goes somewhere small on it. A sticker nobody can trace is a
good sticker and a wasted one.

## Naming

`OPEN` — no brand name chosen. Marty's constraints for candidates:
- Must survive being said out loud in a bar.
- Should gesture at *both* halves (making + consuming) rather than picking one.
- `.com` and a clean Instagram handle available.
- Not a pun that gets old by month four.
